## The constraint (lessons from getting burned before)

- Reed will **not** carry fixed salary that outruns revenue. Past experience:
  **~$7,000/mo payroll for two people in a month that brought in ~$2,000** of
  work. That kind of mismatch is a killer for a shop this size.
- Previous bad hires were often **foisted on him by his former business
  partner**. He's now solo and makes these calls himself — wants to choose well.
- Strong preference for a **variable-cost** arrangement: pay a share of what the
  work brings in, rather than a flat monthly wage, at least in the short term.

---

## Pay structure — IMPORTANT caution

Reed's instinct (pay per job / commission, not fixed wage) is economically
right. But the **1099 contractor / straight-commission version is a legal trap
in California.**

- Under **AB5 / the ABC test**, a worker doing your *core business*
  (digitizing), *at your facility*, *on your equipment* is almost certainly a
  **W-2 employee** in the state's eyes — regardless of the contract or how
  they're paid. Misclassification penalties (back wages, taxes, fines) can far
  exceed a couple of slow-revenue months.

**Recommended path that keeps the variable-cost benefit AND stays compliant:**

- **Part-time, as-needed, hourly W-2** employee, ideally on a **piece rate**
  (per tape / per carousel / per reel) for volume loading work.
- You only put them on the clock when there's a job in the building. No job that
  week → no hours → no cost. Same economics Reed wants, without the $5k-for-$2k
  exposure *or* the misclassification exposure.
- CA allows piece-rate W-2 as long as minimum wage is guaranteed for hours
  worked. A payroll service (e.g. Gusto) sets this up cheaply; one call to a CA
  employment attorney confirms it.

**One-line version:** keep the economics, change the label — as-needed
hourly/piece-rate **W-2**, not 1099 commission.

## Who to hire

The right first hire is someone **genuinely, desperately fascinated with legacy
media formats, restoration, and reviving history** — likely someone training to
be an archivist. That person will happily do repetitive loading work because
they find the *content* fascinating. They exist; the question is where to look.

Note on skills: the three current jobs span audio (cassettes), stills (slides),
and video — mostly **volume loading**, which is learnable. Reed keeps the skilled
restoration; the hire handles throughput.

---

## Where to look (ranked)

1. **AMIA — Association of Moving Image Archivists.** The professional home of
   exactly this person. Post on the **job board**, the **AMIA-L listserv**, and
   reach the **student chapters**. Best first move.
2. **ARSC — Association for Recorded Sound Collections.** For the audio/cassette
   side specifically; an obsessive tape-preservation community.
3. **University programs (Reed already has two as clients — use that):**
   - **UCLA — MA in Moving Image Archive Studies (MIAS).** Local; students need
     hands-on lab experience and relish it.
   - **NYU — Moving Image Archiving & Preservation (MIAP).**
   - **The L. Jeffrey Selznick School of Film Preservation** (George Eastman
     Museum, Rochester) — premier film-preservation training.
4. **Enthusiast underground (self-taught fanatics):** videohelp.com forums,
   Nitrateville, the 8mm Forum, r/VHS, r/AnalogCommunity, r/datahoarder,
   Home Movie Day / Center for Home Movies volunteers.
5. **Local lab-tech pool.** As photochemical/dubbing houses close, skilled
   operators who love the work become available in LA. Blue Collar Post
   Collective; LA post-production Facebook groups.

hand. The site is already a magnet for clients; it can be a magnet for the right
hire too.
